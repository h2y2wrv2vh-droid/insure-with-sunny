Run a Retail Store in Ontario? The Insurance You Actually Need

The holiday shopping season is the busiest time of the year for Ontario retailers. Here is the insurance that actually protects a store, its stock, and its customers when the rush is on.

A retail store runs on its shelves. Every box in the back room and every item on display is money you have already spent, waiting to be sold. The holiday season is when that stock is at its highest and the store is at its fullest, which is also when a single incident can cost the most. If your policy was set up when the store opened and never revisited, it may not match the business you run today. Late November is a good time to check, before the busiest weeks of the year.

Your stock is your business

The first thing to check is whether your policy covers the full value of your inventory, not just the value you had when the policy was written. Stock levels rise sharply before the holidays, and a policy with a low limit can leave a large part of your inventory uncovered if there is a fire, a break-in, or a burst pipe. This is also the season when deliveries pile up in the back room, and goods in transit or temporarily stored off site may need their own coverage. Ask your broker to confirm the limit reflects what is actually on your shelves right now, and whether seasonal increases are built into the policy or need to be added.

When a customer gets hurt in your store

Commercial general liability, usually called CGL, is the policy that responds when someone says your business hurt them or damaged their property. For a retailer, the most common claims are simple ones. A customer slips on a wet floor near the entrance. A shelf tips or a display falls and injures someone. A shopping bag catches on a fixture and a customer trips. These claims are rarely dramatic, but they can be expensive once medical costs and legal fees are added. Your CGL limit should be high enough for the foot traffic you actually get, and your lease may set a minimum limit your landlord expects you to carry, so check both.

The risks that surprise store owners

Some of the costliest retail claims have nothing to do with customers. A burst pipe over a long weekend can soak stock and force a closure. A break-in can take high-value items and leave broken glass and damaged fixtures behind. A power failure can shut down your point-of-sale system on your busiest day. Business interruption coverage is the part of the policy that helps with lost income and extra costs when a covered event forces you to close or operate at reduced capacity, and it is the coverage store owners most often discover too late. If you rely on one supplier for your best-selling lines, a problem at their end can also leave your shelves empty, so ask whether your policy covers that kind of supply disruption.

A short checklist before the holiday rush

Use this week to check a few things. Confirm your stock limit matches what is actually in the store right now. Confirm your liability limit meets what your lease requires. Make sure business interruption coverage is in place and the waiting period makes sense for a store that cannot afford to be closed for long. Check that your alarm and sprinkler systems are working and that your insurer knows about them, because a system that is not maintained can affect a claim. Keep an up-to-date inventory list with photos of high-value items, and keep copies of receipts and supplier invoices somewhere safe. After a loss, that list is what turns a long argument into a quick settlement.
