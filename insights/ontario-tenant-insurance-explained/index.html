Renting in Ontario? Tenant Insurance in Plain English

If you rent your home in Ontario, your landlord's insurance does not protect your belongings or cover you if something goes wrong in your unit. Tenant insurance fills that gap, and it is usually simpler than most renters expect.

What tenant insurance actually is

Tenant insurance, also called renters insurance, is a policy made for people who rent. It covers three things in plain terms. First, your belongings, such as furniture, clothes, electronics and kitchen things. Second, your responsibility if you accidentally cause damage or someone gets hurt in your unit. Third, the extra cost of living somewhere else while your place cannot be lived in after a covered loss.

Your landlord's policy does not cover your stuff

This is the misunderstanding that catches renters out. The building owner's policy typically covers the building itself, the walls, the roof and the shared systems. It typically does not cover your furniture, your clothes, your electronics or anything else you brought with you.

If a fire, a burst pipe or a break-in damages your belongings, that loss is usually yours to carry unless you have your own policy. The same applies to liability. If a guest is hurt in your unit, or your bathtub overflows into the unit below, the claim can land on you, not on your landlord.

The three parts, in plain language

Contents cover is the first part. It typically helps replace or repair your belongings if they are damaged or stolen in a covered event, such as a fire, a break-in or certain kinds of water damage. When you set it up, you pick a total amount that should reflect what it would cost to replace what you own, not what you paid for it years ago.

Liability cover is the second part. If someone is hurt in your unit, or you accidentally damage the building or a neighbour's unit, this is the part that typically helps with the claim and the legal costs that can come with it. For renters, this is often the most important part, because a single liability claim can cost far more than replacing a couch.

Extra living costs are the third part. If a covered loss makes your unit unlivable, this part typically helps with the extra cost of a hotel or a short-term rental, and with meals out while you cannot cook at home.

What it usually does not cover

No policy covers everything, so it helps to know the limits before you need them. Damage you cause on purpose is not covered. Your roommate's belongings are usually not covered unless they are named on the policy, so each roommate typically needs their own cover. Valuable items such as jewellery or collectibles often have a set limit, and you may need to list them separately if they are worth more than that limit. Some kinds of water damage, such as water coming in from outside the building, may need extra cover, so it is worth asking what your policy includes.

Four things to check before you buy

First, add up what you own. Walk through your place with your phone and note the big items, then add a rough value for the rest. Most people own more than they think once they count everything.

Second, ask about the liability limit. This is the most the policy will typically pay on a liability claim, and it is the number that matters most if something serious happens.

Third, know your deductible. That is the amount you pay out of pocket before the policy starts paying on a contents claim, and a higher deductible usually means a lower premium.

Fourth, ask your landlord what they need. Some landlords in Ontario ask for proof of tenant insurance before you move in, and some leases require it. If yours does, ask what limit they expect so your policy matches it.

What this means for your next renewal or move

Tenant insurance protects the things you use every day, plus the liability that can follow you after an accident in your own home.
