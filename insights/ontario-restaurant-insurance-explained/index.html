Running a Restaurant in Ontario? The Insurance You Actually Need

Hot kitchens, busy dining rooms and late nights bring risks most businesses never face. Here is the insurance that typically protects an Ontario restaurant, in plain language.

What this means for you

If you run a restaurant in Ontario, your business faces risks most others never think about: hot kitchens, busy dining rooms, and staff working late. The right insurance package covers the everyday accidents and the rare bad day, so one incident does not close your doors for good.

A restaurant policy is usually a package, not one coverage

Most restaurants do not buy one single policy. They buy a package that bundles the main protections together, then add the extras that fit how they actually operate. The package typically has three parts at its core: cover for your property, cover for your liability, and cover for lost income if you have to close after a covered loss.

That last part matters for a restaurant more than for most businesses. If a kitchen fire shuts you down for a month, you lose the food sales you would have made every day. This kind of cover typically helps with the income you lose and some of the ongoing costs while you rebuild.

Liability: the part that protects you when a customer is hurt

Liability cover is the part that typically helps if a customer is hurt or their property is damaged and your restaurant is found responsible. That can be a slip on a wet floor, a burn from a hot plate, or a customer who gets sick after a meal.

If you serve alcohol, ask about liquor liability. Serving alcohol brings its own risk, and the cover for it is often a separate part of the policy, so make sure your policy includes it and that the limit fits how much you serve.

Your property and equipment

Your property cover typically protects the building if you own it, plus the things inside that keep the kitchen running: ovens, fridges, freezers, prep tables, and dining room furniture.

Two things are easy to miss. First, food spoilage. If a fridge or freezer breaks down, you can lose a lot of stock in one night, and some policies include cover for spoiled food while others do not, so ask. Second, make sure the total amount on your equipment reflects what it would cost to replace it today, not what you paid years ago.

What it usually does not cover

No policy covers everything. Damage you cause on purpose is not covered. Flood water coming in from outside the building often needs extra cover. If your staff use their own cars to deliver food, their personal auto policy usually does not cover them while they are working, and your restaurant policy may not either.

Staff injuries are also separate. In Ontario, workplace injuries are usually handled through WSIB, not through your restaurant insurance package. If you have staff, make sure your WSIB registration and your insurance are both in order.

Four things to check before you buy or renew

First, walk through your restaurant and list what you would need to replace after a fire: equipment, furniture, stock, and the fit-out. That list is what your property amount should reflect.

Second, check your liability limit. This is the most the policy will typically pay on a liability claim, and it is the number that matters most if a customer is seriously hurt.

Third, ask about closing costs. If a covered loss shuts you down, how long does the cover pay for? A week of cover is very different from a month.

Fourth, tell your broker how you actually operate. Takeout, delivery, catering, patio service, and late hours each change the risk. A policy written for a small lunch spot will not fit a restaurant doing late-night delivery, and the cheapest quote is often the one that left something out.

What this means for your restaurant

Restaurant insurance is not about buying the most cover. It is about matching the cover to the way your restaurant actually runs, so a bad night stays a bad night instead of becoming the end of the business.
