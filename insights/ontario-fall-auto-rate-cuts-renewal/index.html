Some Ontario Auto Insurers Are Cutting Rates This Fall: What It Means for Your Renewal

After years of climbing premiums, Ontario's auto insurance regulator has approved average rate decreases for a dozen insurers this fall. The catch: the approved cut is an average, and your renewal depends on your insurer, your effective date, and your own file.

A rare piece of good news in auto insurance

After years of steady increases, Ontario drivers are finally seeing rate cuts from the regulator. Ontario's insurance regulator, the Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario (FSRA), has approved average rate decreases for a dozen private-passenger auto insurance filings covering renewals this fall. The list comes from reporting by Carsaga for drivingtest.ca, published October 2, 2026, with the filings verified directly against FSRA's public Auto Rate Approvals database.

The cuts do not erase the increases behind them, but if your renewal lands in the fall window, you should know which side of the line your insurer is on.

Which insurers are cutting, and by how much

The approved decreases range from modest to meaningful. The Personal Insurance Company leads with an average decrease of 5.30% for renewals effective November 28, 2026. Farm Mutual Re, filing for Ontario mutual insurers, is next at 4.89% for December 1 renewals, followed by Heartland Farm Mutual at 4.62% for renewals effective August 15. Certas Direct and Certas Home and Auto each filed 2.00% for November 28 renewals. Aviva General Insurance filed 1.64% effective September 1, Allstate Insurance Company of Canada 1.64% effective September 15, Pembridge 1.45% on the same date, Definity 1.31% effective September 1, and CAA Insurance 0.12% effective December 1. Two further decreases are scheduled to take effect January 1, 2027.

Across every filing approved in 2026, the weighted-average approved change is minus 0.89%, the first net-negative year since 2021. That figure covers both decreases and increases, so it reflects the whole market, not just the cut list.

What the cuts are really worth

A cut that sounds small is still real money, but it does not erase the increases behind it. FSRA's figures put the average Ontario private-passenger premium at $1,927 in June 2024 and $2,164 in October 2025, a gain of $237, or 12.3%, in sixteen months. At that average, the cuts return roughly $35 a year for Aviva and Allstate customers, about $100 for Heartland and Farm Mutual Re customers, and about $115 for customers of The Personal, per the drivingtest.ca analysis. Even the largest cut claws back less than half of one year's average increase.

The broader trend explains why cuts are landing now. Statistics Canada reported in June that passenger-vehicle insurance premiums rose 23.9% between December 2019 and December 2025, faster than the 21.0% rise in overall inflation. But Applied Systems' Rating Index, which tracks broker quotes, found Ontario quoted premiums rose 11.8% year over year in the first quarter of 2026, down from 17.1% the quarter before. Cost pressure is easing, and the regulator's approvals are following it down.

Why your renewal could still go up

An approved rate cut does not guarantee a lower bill. FSRA says so on its own database: the approved change is the expected average across all of an insurer's renewals, and an individual premium may go up, go down, or stay the same depending on the driver's record, vehicle, location, and choice of coverage.

Insurers reweight territories, vehicle classes, and rating factors inside that average, so your postal code or car model can absorb more than the average while others absorb less. Timing matters too: the cut applies only to renewals on or after the effective date, so a customer of The Personal renewing October 15 stays on the old rate until the November 28 cut kicks in. And anything that changed on your own file since last renewal, an at-fault claim, a conviction, a move, a new vehicle, or an added driver, reprices your premium independently of the average.

Nor is every insurer cutting. Approved increases in the same window run up to 8.70% for the Facility Association, the shared facility covering drivers who cannot get coverage in the regular market, while Co-operators General, Echelon, Optimum, and Peel Mutual froze rates at 0.0%.

The July 1 accident-benefits reform sits in the background

On July 1, 2026, nine accident benefits that used to be automatic in every Ontario policy became optional, including income replacement, caregiver, housekeeping, death and funeral, and non-earner benefits. Some coverage of the rate cuts has asked whether the reform is behind them. The honest answer is that nobody can say: most cut filings were approved before the reform took effect, and FSRA has attributed no specific filing to it.

What does affect you directly: renewal notices now carry opt-out choices for those nine benefits. Brokerage estimates cited with the reporting put the saving from declining all of them at roughly $75 to $100 a year, most of it from income replacement. That is real money, but weigh it against what you would give up. If you are self-employed, a gig worker, or the main earner in your household, income replacement after a serious collision could matter far more than the annual saving. Talk it through with a broker before you tick any box.

Your fall renewal checklist

First, find the legal insurer name on your policy documents or renewal letter, because brands and legal entities differ: Sonnet policies are issued by Definity, "The Personal" is the Personal Insurance Company, and Pembridge is Allstate's brokerage channel. Second, look the company up in FSRA's free public Auto Rate Approvals database and note the approved change and the renewal effective date. Third, compare that effective date with your own renewal date: if yours falls before it, the cut applies next cycle, not this one. Fourth, remember that insurers must send your renewal notice 30 to 60 days before expiry, which is your window to shop competing quotes before anything locks in. Fifth, if your letter shows an increase while your insurer's filing shows a cut, ask which rating factors moved. Individual movement inside the average is normal, but a large divergence is worth a competing quote.

Rate cuts in Ontario are real but uneven. Your renewal letter, matched against the regulator's database, tells you which side you are on.
