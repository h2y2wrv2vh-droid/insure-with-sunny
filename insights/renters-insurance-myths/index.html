5 Renters Insurance Myths Ontario Tenants Still Believe

I talk to renters every week who are one burst pipe away from learning these lessons the hard way. Here are the five myths I hear most often, and the truth behind each one.

Renters insurance is one of the best values in all of insurance, and one of the least understood. A huge share of Ontario tenants do not carry it, usually because of one of these five myths. Let me take them apart.

Myth 1: My landlord's insurance covers my belongings

This is the big one, and it is completely wrong. Your landlord's policy covers the building: the walls, the roof, the structure. It does not cover a single thing you own. If a fire, burst pipe or break-in destroys your furniture, clothes and electronics, the landlord's insurer pays the landlord for the building and pays you nothing. Your belongings need your own policy.

Myth 2: Renters insurance is expensive

It is typically one of the most affordable policies you can buy, often costing less per month than a streaming subscription or two. For that you get coverage for your belongings, liability protection, and additional living expenses if your unit becomes unlivable. Per dollar of protection, it is hard to beat.

Myth 3: I do not own enough stuff to insure

Almost everyone underestimates this. Walk through your place mentally and add it up: bed, couch, TV, laptop, phone, clothes, kitchen gear, sports equipment. Most people are surprised to find the total lands in the tens of thousands. Could you replace all of it tomorrow out of pocket? If not, you own enough to insure.

Myth 4: My roommate's policy covers me too

Insurance covers the people named on the policy, not everyone with a key to the front door. Your roommate's renters policy protects your roommate's belongings and your roommate's liability. It does nothing for you. Each tenant needs their own policy, and they are cheap enough that sharing one was never the point.

Myth 5: I only need it for fire or theft

The belongings coverage gets the attention, but liability is the quiet powerhouse of a renters policy. If you accidentally flood the unit below you, start a kitchen fire that damages the building, or your dog bites a visitor, liability coverage responds. And if a covered loss makes your apartment unlivable, additional living expenses coverage pays for a hotel while it is repaired. Those protections matter far more often than people expect.

What is actually covered: a quick tour

A standard renters policy has three main parts. Personal property covers your belongings against insured perils like fire, theft, and water damage from burst pipes, up to your chosen limit. Liability protects you if you accidentally injure someone or damage their property, which is the coverage that handles the flooded-downstairs-neighbour scenario. Additional living expenses pays for a hotel and meals if a covered loss forces you out of your unit. Together, those three cover the realistic worst cases of renting life.

How to file a renters claim

If something happens, the process is straightforward: document the damage with photos, make a list of what was lost or damaged, and call your broker or insurer promptly. For theft, file a police report as well. Keep receipts for any emergency spending, like a hotel stay, since those may be reimbursable. The renters who have the smoothest claims are the ones who photographed their belongings once on a quiet Sunday and never thought about it again until they needed to.

What renters insurance does not cover

No policy covers everything, so know the edges. High-value items like expensive jewelry or collectibles often have sub-limits, and may need to be scheduled separately. Overland flooding usually requires an add-on, just like with home insurance. Your roommate's belongings are not covered, business inventory stored at home generally is not, and damage you cause intentionally never is. None of these are deal-breakers; they are just details to sort out when you buy the policy, not after a loss.

If you rent, do this

  • Get your own policy. Do not rely on a landlord's or roommate's coverage.
  • Photograph your belongings once, so a claim is not a memory test.
  • Check your lease. Many Ontario landlords now require tenants to carry renters insurance.
  • Bundle it. Adding renters to your auto policy with the same insurer often earns a discount on both.

Renters insurance is a small policy that prevents life-altering bills. If you rent in Ontario and do not have it, that is the single easiest insurance gap to close, and it takes one phone call.
