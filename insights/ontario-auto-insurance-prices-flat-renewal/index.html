Ontario Auto Insurance Prices Have Stopped Climbing. Your Renewal Still Might Go Up

Auto insurance prices in Ontario have stopped climbing, according to new figures from Statistics Canada. But repair bills are still rising, and that gap is why your next renewal is still worth a close look.

Here is some good news for Ontario drivers: the price of auto insurance in Ontario has stopped climbing. New figures from Statistics Canada show the price index for passenger vehicle insurance in the province was unchanged in the year to October 2026. A year earlier, it was up 8.3 percent. If your renewal has felt like a punch in the gut for the past few years, this is the first real pause. But do not celebrate just yet. Your own renewal can still go up, and the reason is sitting in the repair shop.

What the numbers actually say

The price index is an average across the whole province, not a promise about your policy. Statistics Canada also reports that the cost of parts, maintenance, and repairs in Ontario rose 4.2 percent over the same year, the fastest pace in more than a year. Cars cost more to fix, and a bigger repair bill means a bigger claim. The regulator's own numbers tell a similar story. Since July 1, 2026, Ontario's insurance regulator, FSRA, has approved 21 private passenger auto rate filings: eight were decreases, seven were no change, and six were increases. The middle figure, the median, was zero. Most of those approved changes take effect on renewals from December 2026 to March 2027, so many drivers will see the effect at their next renewal, not the last one.

Why your renewal can still go up

A flat average does not mean a flat renewal for you. An approved rate change is an average across one insurer's whole book of customers. Your renewal reflects your own driving record, your car, where you live, and the coverages you picked. If you had a claim, added a young driver, or moved, your price can move even when the provincewide average does not. And the repair-cost gap matters. When parts and labour keep rising while prices stay flat, something has to give, and over time that usually means pressure on rates. The pause in prices is real, but it is not a guarantee that your next renewal will match the last one.

Check what changed on your policy in July

There is another reason to read your renewal carefully. On July 1, 2026, Ontario changed which accident benefits are mandatory. Medical, rehabilitation, and attendant care benefits stayed mandatory. But income replacement, non-earner, and caregiver benefits became optional coverages that insurers must offer. Your existing policy keeps its previous coverage until renewal, unless you agreed in writing to change it. That means your next renewal is the moment your choices take effect, so it is worth knowing what you chose and what it means if you are hurt in a crash.

What to check before your renewal

When your renewal arrives, spend ten minutes on it before you pay.

  • Compare the price with last year, and check whether any coverage changed
  • Look at the accident benefits you chose, and make sure they match what you want
  • Check your deductible, the amount you pay first on a claim, and whether it still makes sense for you
  • Tell your broker about anything that changed: a new driver, a different commute, a car you no longer drive
  • Ask for a second look before renewal day, not after, when your options are narrower

Ontario auto prices have finally stopped climbing, and that is worth noting after years of increases. But an average is not your renewal. A quick check now can catch a coverage change or a price move while there is still time to do something about it.
