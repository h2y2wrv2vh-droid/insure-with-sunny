Frozen Pipes and Winter Claims: How to Protect Your Home

Every deep freeze brings the same calls: water pouring through a ceiling from a burst pipe. Most of these claims are preventable. Here is what I tell homeowners every fall before the cold arrives.

Water expands when it freezes, and it does so with enough force to split copper and plastic alike. When that ice thaws, the water goes wherever gravity takes it: through ceilings, down walls, into floors. I have seen single burst-pipe claims run into the tens of thousands of dollars. Almost all of them started with something preventable.

Keep the heat on, even when you are away

The number one cause of frozen pipes is a home that got too cold. Never turn your heat off entirely in winter, even if you are away for a week. A modest, consistent temperature keeps pipes above freezing. The small heating bill is nothing next to a burst pipe.

Insulate the vulnerable spots

Pipes in attics, crawl spaces, garages and exterior walls freeze first because they sit in the coldest parts of the house. Foam pipe insulation is inexpensive and takes an afternoon to install. Pay special attention to any plumbing on outside walls, like kitchen sinks, which are classic freeze points.

Shut off and drain exterior water lines

Garden hoses, outdoor taps and irrigation lines should be disconnected, shut off from inside, and drained before the first hard freeze. A hose left connected can trap water in the pipe, freeze, and burst the line inside your wall, where you will not notice until spring.

Know where your main shutoff is

If a pipe does burst, every minute of flowing water multiplies the damage. Everyone in your household should know where the main water shutoff valve is and how to turn it. If you do not know where yours is, go find it today. It takes two minutes and it is the highest-value two minutes in homeownership.

Going away? Have someone check in

Many home insurance policies have conditions about how often someone must check on your home when you are away during heating season. A furnace can fail on day two of a two-week trip, and an unchecked home can freeze solid. Arrange for a neighbour, friend or family member to stop in regularly, and confirm what your policy requires.

Open cabinet doors on exterior walls

During extreme cold snaps, opening the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls lets warm room air reach the pipes. It is a small, free step that helps exactly where freezing is most likely.

Smart home helpers: leak detectors and auto shutoffs

Technology has made prevention easier. Inexpensive water leak detectors placed near water heaters, washing machines and under sinks can alert your phone the moment they sense moisture. More advanced systems can automatically shut off your main water supply when a major leak is detected. Some insurers look favourably on these devices, but the real payoff is catching a leak in minutes instead of discovering it after a weekend away.

What insurance covers, and what it does not

A sudden, accidental burst pipe is typically a covered claim under a standard home policy, including the resulting water damage. But damage from slow, gradual leaks, or from pipes that froze because the heat was off or the home was unoccupied without proper precautions, is a different story. Insurers expect reasonable maintenance. Prevention is not just good sense; it protects your coverage too.

After the thaw: check for damage

When spring arrives, do a walk-around before you forget winter happened. Look for water stains on ceilings, musty smells in the basement, and cracked caulking around windows where ice may have forced its way in. Small leaks caught in April are cheap; the same leaks discovered in August can mean mould remediation. A seasonal inspection habit is one of the cheapest forms of insurance there is.

Your fall plumbing checklist

  • Disconnect hoses and shut off exterior water lines before the first freeze.
  • Insulate exposed pipes in attics, crawl spaces and garages.
  • Locate your main shutoff and show everyone in the house.
  • Arrange check-ins if you travel during heating season, and know your policy's requirements.
  • Keep the heat on. Always.

Winter will do what winter does. The homeowners who sail through it are the ones who spent one October afternoon on prevention. Be one of them, and if you are ever unsure whether your policy covers a winter-related loss, ask before the season starts rather than after the damage is done.
