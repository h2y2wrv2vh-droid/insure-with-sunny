What to Do After a Car Accident in Ontario

Accidents are stressful, and the minutes after one matter more than people realize. Here is the exact sequence I tell my clients to follow, so a bad day does not turn into a bad claim.

Even a minor collision floods you with adrenaline, and adrenaline makes people forgetful. That is why it helps to know the steps before you need them. Print this mentally, because one day you might be standing on the shoulder of the 401 trying to remember what comes first.

1. Safety first, everything else second

Check yourself and your passengers for injuries. If anyone is hurt, call 911 immediately. If the vehicles are drivable and it is safe, move them out of traffic and turn on your hazard lights. Your health and everyone else's comes before bumpers and paperwork, always.

2. Know when police need to be involved

In Ontario, you must report a collision to police if anyone is injured, if there is significant property damage, or if a criminal act like impaired driving is involved. For collisions with combined damage over $2,000 and no injuries, you generally report to a Collision Reporting Centre instead of calling 911. When in doubt, call; they will direct you.

3. Exchange information

Collect the other driver's name, address, phone number, driver's licence number, licence plate, and insurance company and policy number. Give them yours in return. Keep it civil and factual. This is not the time to debate fault.

4. Document everything

Photograph all vehicles from multiple angles, the surrounding road, traffic signs, skid marks, and any visible injuries. Note the time, location, weather and road conditions. If there are witnesses, ask for their names and numbers. Memories fade and stories change; photos and notes do not.

5. Do not admit fault at the scene

It is natural to want to apologize, but fault is a legal determination, not a roadside one. Stick to the facts when speaking to the other driver and to police. Saying "I'm sorry" can be interpreted as an admission, so keep the conversation to exchanging information.

6. Notify your insurer promptly

Call your broker or insurer as soon as you reasonably can. Policies require timely notice of a claim, and early reporting lets the claims process start while evidence is fresh. Have your policy number, the police or collision report number, and your photos ready.

7. Understand how Ontario claims work

Ontario uses a direct compensation system: when you are not at fault, your own insurer generally handles the damage to your vehicle rather than you chasing the other driver's company. Separately, accident benefits coverage provides medical, rehabilitation and income-related benefits regardless of who caused the collision. Your broker or adjuster can explain how both apply to your situation.

What happens after you report the claim

Once you report, an adjuster is assigned and will walk you through the next steps: getting repair estimates, arranging a rental vehicle if your policy includes that coverage, and handling the fault determination. In Ontario, fault is assessed using a set of rules applied to the facts of the collision, not by whoever argues loudest. Cooperate with the process, keep copies of everything, and loop in your broker if anything feels off. A good broker advocates for you through the claim, not just at the sale.

Take care of yourself, not just the car

Vehicle damage gets the attention, but your health comes first. See a doctor promptly even if you feel fine, since injuries like whiplash often surface days later. Ontario's accident benefits coverage provides medical and rehabilitation benefits regardless of fault, but claims go more smoothly when treatment starts early and is well documented. Keep every receipt and appointment record.

Keep a claim folder

From the day of the accident, keep everything in one place: the police or collision report number, photos, tow receipts, repair estimates, medical appointments and any correspondence with the insurer. Claims can take weeks to resolve, and the organized file always moves faster than the shoebox of random papers. Your future self will thank you.

Keep in your glove box (or phone)

  • Your broker's phone number and your policy number, saved where you can find them stressed.
  • A pen and notepad as backup, because phones die at the worst moments.
  • The sequence: safety, 911 if injuries, exchange info, photos, report, notify insurer.
  • Never admit fault at the scene; stick to facts.

No one plans to need this guide. But the drivers who handle accidents best are the ones who thought about it once on a calm day, exactly like you are doing right now.
