Fake Pink Slips Are Circulating in Ontario. Here Is How to Check Your Auto Insurance Is Real

Ontario's insurance regulator has warned drivers that a website was selling fake auto insurance pink slips. If you bought your pink slip online, here is how to check your coverage is real, before you need it.

If you bought your auto insurance pink slip from a website instead of a broker or an insurer you know, this warning is for you. A pink slip is the card that proves you have auto insurance, and Ontario's regulator has found that fake ones were being sold online. A card that looks real is not the same as real coverage, and the difference only shows up when you need it most.

What happened

On November 10, Ontario's insurance regulator, the Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario (FSRA), warned drivers about a website that was selling fake auto insurance pink slips. The site used the Definity Insurance name and charged a flat fee for the cards. FSRA says the site was not licensed to sell insurance in Ontario and was not connected to the real Definity Insurance Company. The website has since been taken down.

Why a fake pink slip is a real problem

A pink slip is only useful if there is a real policy behind it. A fake card means no insurer will pay if you are in a crash. That leaves the driver personally responsible for the cost of damage or injuries, and those costs can run into the hundreds of thousands of dollars. That is the warning from Jordan Solway, FSRA's executive vice-president for property and casualty insurance, who said driving with a fake pink slip could have significant consequences. A fake card can also mean a fine for driving without insurance, even if the driver believed they were covered.

How to check your coverage is real

Three quick checks take only a few minutes, and they are worth doing if you bought your policy anywhere unusual. First, call the insurer or brokerage named on your card and ask them to confirm your policy is active. Have your policy number ready when you call. Second, check that the seller is licensed. FSRA's website lets you verify an insurer or agent, and the Registered Insurance Brokers of Ontario website lets you check a broker's licence. Third, look at how the policy was sold to you. Real auto insurance in Ontario involves an application, questions about you and your car, and a proper policy document. Be wary of a flat fee with no questions asked, no paperwork, and payment only by e-transfer or gift card.

What to do if you bought one

If you bought a pink slip through that website, FSRA says to contact police and report the fraud, and to call the insurer or brokerage listed on the card to check whether you have any valid coverage. Do not keep driving on a card you now doubt. Get real coverage in place first, then speak to a licensed broker about what you need and what it should cost.

Buying auto insurance safely

The safest way to buy auto insurance is through a licensed broker or directly from a licensed insurer, where you get a real application, a policy number, and documents you can verify. If a deal comes through a social media ad or a website you have never heard of, pause before you pay. Check the name against FSRA's list, ask for the broker's licence number, and never pay for a pink slip on its own. A real policy is a contract, not a card you buy by itself.

A pink slip should be the easiest part of driving, not a gamble. If you are not sure your auto policy is real, or you bought coverage somewhere that felt off, message me for a no-pressure second look. A quick check now is far cheaper than finding out after a crash.
