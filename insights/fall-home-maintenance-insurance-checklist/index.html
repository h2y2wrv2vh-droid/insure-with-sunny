Fall Home Maintenance That Can Save Your Insurance Claim

October is the last comfortable month to take care of your house. A few weekends of basic maintenance are the cheapest insurance you can buy, and your home policy expects it. Here is the fall checklist that keeps small problems from becoming big claims.

October is the last comfortable month of the year to take care of your house. Once the cold settles in, the ladder work, the yard work and the furnace work all get harder and more expensive. A few October weekends spent on basic maintenance are the cheapest insurance you can buy, and your actual insurance policy will back you up on that. Home insurance is written to cover sudden and accidental loss, not damage that builds up because the roof was already failing or the eavestroughs were packed with leaves. When an adjuster looks at a claim, one of the first questions is whether the homeowner did the reasonable upkeep a homeowner is expected to do.

Water finds every weak spot first

Almost every major fall and winter home insurance claim starts with water getting where it does not belong. Start with the eavestroughs. Clean them out and make sure downspouts push water at least a few feet away from the foundation, not straight down beside it. Clogged gutters overflow at the fascia board, water runs behind the siding, and by February you have ice, rot and a leak that came out of nowhere. It did not come out of nowhere.

Walk around the house during the next heavy rain and watch where water goes. Soil should slope away from the foundation, not toward it. If your basement has a sump pump, test it now, not when the spring melt arrives. And while you are at it, know whether your policy includes overland water and sewer backup endorsements. After a summer of flooding across Ontario, including the September storms that left parts of the GTA under water, the homes that recovered fastest were the ones whose owners had already confirmed those coverages before they needed them.

Check the roof before it is covered in snow

You do not need to walk your roof. Use binoculars from the driveway. Look for shingles that are curled, cracked or missing, and check the flashing around chimneys and vents. Inside the attic, look for daylight where it should not be and water stains on the underside of the roof deck. A slow roof leak found in October is a repair job. The same leak found in January becomes a claim, and possibly a complicated one if the insurer can show the roof was already in poor condition. Most policies expect roofs to be maintained in good repair, and a roof that is visibly at the end of its life is one of the most common reasons claims get reduced or denied.

Service the heating and clear the fire risks

Get the furnace serviced before it runs all winter. A technician will clean it, check the heat exchanger and make sure the venting is right, which is worth doing for both safety and efficiency. Replace the furnace filter now and put a reminder on your phone to do it again mid-winter. While you are at it, check the smoke and carbon monoxide detectors, replace the batteries, and make sure you have working extinguishers on each floor of the house. Clean the dryer vent too. Lint buildup is a genuine fire hazard, it takes twenty minutes, and it is exactly the kind of neglected maintenance that looks bad on a fire claim.

If you have a wood-burning fireplace, October is the time to get the chimney inspected and swept. Creosote buildup causes chimney fires, and insurers take notice when a fire starts in an unmaintained flue. Gas fireplaces need a periodic check as well, even though they are lower maintenance.

Trim the trees and secure the exterior

Dead or overhanging branches are a winter storm away from becoming a hole in your roof or a crushed fence. Trim back anything close to the house now, while the ground is firm and crews are easy to book. Store or secure anything that becomes a projectile in high winds: patio furniture, grills, planters. If you have a pool, close it properly. And if your garage or shed holds anything valuable, make sure the locks actually work, because seasonal theft from outbuildings tends to rise as days get shorter.

Document everything, because claims run on proof

Here is the part most people skip. Take ten minutes and photograph your roof, your basement, your electrical panel and your big-ticket items. Save receipts for the furnace service and the chimney sweep. If you ever file a claim, this folder is the difference between a smooth process and a long argument. Insurers settle documented losses faster. Photos also help your broker make sure your contents coverage is adequate before you need it. A phone full of dated photos costs nothing and quietly does more for a future claim than any rider on the policy.

The October weekend checklist

  • Clean the eavestroughs and extend downspouts away from the foundation.
  • Inspect the roof with binoculars, and check the attic for stains.
  • Service the furnace and change the filter.
  • Test the sump pump and check the grading around the basement.
  • Replace detector batteries and clean the dryer vent.
  • Trim overhanging branches and secure outdoor items.
  • Photograph everything and save maintenance receipts.

Maintenance protects you twice. It stops small problems from becoming insurance claims, and when a claim does happen, it keeps the conversation simple. While you are in fall-prep mode, it is also a good moment to review your coverage itself. Do you have overland water and sewer backup? Is your contents coverage still right? Those are quick questions for your broker, and October is the right month to ask them.
