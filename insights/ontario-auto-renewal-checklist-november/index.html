Your Ontario Auto Renewal Is Coming. Here Is What to Check Before You Pay It

Some Ontario insurers have approved lower average rates this fall, and your renewal date decides whether any of it reaches your bill. Before you accept the new price, here are the checks that actually matter.

Some rates are going down. Yours might not be

After years of mostly rising premiums, nine Ontario insurers now have approved average rate reductions taking effect on renewals between August and November 2026. The reductions range from 0.23 percent at Wawanesa to 5.30 percent at The Personal Insurance Company. Heartland Farm Mutual was approved for a 4.62 percent average reduction effective August 15, Aviva General for 1.64 percent effective September 1, and Certas Direct and Certas Home and Auto for 2.00 percent each effective November 28.

Those numbers are averages across each insurer's whole Ontario business. They are not a promise that your own bill will fall by the same amount. Ontario's regulator, FSRA, says approved rate changes may not affect all customers, and your premium can also move because of your vehicle, your driving record, where you live, how much you drive, and the coverage you carry. A driver with an insurer on the rate cut list can still see a higher renewal if something in their own situation changed.

Your renewal date decides whether the cut reaches you

An approved rate change applies to renewals effective from its date. That detail matters more than the headline percentage. If your policy renews before November 28, the Certas and Personal filings typically would not be reflected in that renewal yet. If it renews after, they typically would be, though your own premium can still move for other reasons.

So the first thing to check is the renewal date printed on your renewal notice, then the insurer named on it. FSRA keeps a public database of approved rate changes, and you can look up your own insurer there to see the latest filing and its effective date. Comparing that date with your renewal date tells you whether the filing is even in play for your next bill.

Check your accident benefits while you are at it

Ontario changed its auto accident benefits on July 1, 2026. Medical, rehabilitation and attendant care benefits are still mandatory on every policy. Several other benefits became optional, including income replacement, caregiver benefits, housekeeping and home maintenance, and death and funeral benefits. For new policies and renewals, drivers can choose whether to keep them.

For most existing customers, a policy typically renews with the same coverage unless the driver agrees in writing to a change. That means your renewal notice is worth reading carefully. Removing optional benefits can lower your premium, but it also means giving up protection that could matter after a serious accident. Before you drop anything, it is worth checking what workplace or private benefits you already have, and what gap would be left behind.

Three checks before you accept the renewal

First, compare the new premium with last year's, line by line. Look at the total, and look at the coverage amounts and deductibles beside it. A lower total is only a saving if the coverage stayed the same.

Second, if you shop around, compare like with like. A quote from another insurer can look cheaper simply because it carries less coverage, a higher deductible, or fewer listed drivers. Match the coverage, the deductibles, the drivers and the limits before you compare the prices.

Third, check the details the insurer has on file for you. Your address, your vehicle, the drivers in your household and your annual mileage all feed into the price. A move, a new car, a teen driver with a G2, or a ticket can shift your premium more than any insurer-wide rate filing. If something on the renewal notice is out of date, get it corrected before the policy renews.

What this means for your next bill

The fall rate cuts are real, but they are targeted. Some drivers will see a lower renewal, some will not, and the difference usually comes down to the insurer, the renewal date, and the driver's own details. The Ontario average premium was 2,164 dollars a year as of October 2025, according to FSRA, and it had climbed from 1,927 dollars in June 2024. Against that backdrop, a few percentage points from one filing is helpful, but it is not the whole story.

Your renewal notice is the number that matters. Read it before the renewal date, check the coverage as well as the price, and ask questions while there is still time to change something.
