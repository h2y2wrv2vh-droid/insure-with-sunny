Hamilton Is Offering $2,000 to Flood Victims. Here Is What It Does Not Cover.

A one-time grant helps with cleanup after a brutal summer of storms. But a flooded basement can cost ten times that, and most standard home policies leave the biggest gaps exactly where the water comes in.

The City of Hamilton is offering a one-time compassionate grant of up to $2,000 to residents whose homes were damaged in the rainstorms of July and August 2026. If your basement took on water this summer, that money is worth claiming. It is also worth understanding what it does not do.

Two thousand dollars is real help with cleanup costs, ruined furniture and emergency repairs. It is not a rebuild. A single flooded basement in Ontario commonly costs $20,000 to $40,000 or more to restore once you count flooring, drywall, electrical and mould remediation. The gap between a grant and the actual bill is exactly where insurance is supposed to step in.

The coverage gap most homeowners do not know they have

Here is the part that surprises people. A standard home insurance policy typically covers wind and hail damage, but overland flooding, water that enters your home from outside, is usually an optional add-on. Sewer backup, water forced up through drains when storm systems overload, needs its own endorsement too. After the September 2 storm that left cars submerged on the Don Valley Parkway, the advice from the industry was blunt: without those two add-ons, many homeowners ended up paying out of pocket.

Hamilton is not alone in dealing with this. St. Catharines just opened a flood impact registry to support its push for provincial disaster assistance after its own summer flooding. And Ontario's Disaster Recovery Assistance program states plainly that it does not replace insurance. Applicants are expected to go through their private insurer first.

Claims are slow right now, which makes preparation matter more

There is another wrinkle. With adjusters and restoration crews stretched thin by the September storms, claims are taking longer than usual to resolve across the GTA. When the system is backlogged, the cheapest claim is the one you never have to file, and the fastest payout goes to the homeowner with documentation ready.

What to do this week

  • Pull out your home policy and look for the words "overland water" and "sewer backup." If neither appears, you very likely do not have them.
  • Know your deductible before you need it. Most Ontario deductibles run $500 to $1,000 or more, and water endorsements sometimes carry their own.
  • Photograph your basement and big-ticket items now. Documentation speeds up every claim.
  • If Hamilton's storms hit you, apply for the grant, then call your broker about the rest.

Grants and disaster programs are a safety net with holes in it. The right endorsements typically cost a fraction of a single flood. If you are not sure what your policy actually covers, finding out is literally what I do all day.
