In a Car Accident in Ontario? The First Things to Do at the Scene and After

A crash is stressful even when nobody is hurt. Knowing the few steps to take at the scene, and in the days after, can protect you, your claim, and your premium.

If anyone is hurt, call 911 right away. Do not move someone who is injured unless they are in immediate danger, like a fire or traffic coming toward them. If you can do so safely, move your car out of traffic, turn on your hazard lights, and stay at the scene. Leaving the scene of a crash is an offence in Ontario, even if the damage looks minor.

Call the police when it matters

Call 911 if anyone is injured, if you think the other driver is impaired, if the other driver leaves the scene, or if a crime may be involved. For other crashes, the rule is simpler than most drivers think. If the total damage looks like more than $2,000, or anyone is hurt, the crash has to be reported to police. In many Ontario cities, that means going to a Collision Reporting Centre, usually within 24 hours, if police did not come to the scene. If police do attend, ask for the officer's name, badge number, and the incident number. Your insurer will ask for it later.

Swap information and gather proof

Exchange names, phone numbers, licence plate numbers, and insurance details with the other driver. Take photos of the damage to both cars, where the cars ended up, the road, and the weather. If anyone saw what happened, ask for their name and number. Write down the time, the place, and what you remember while it is fresh. And do not say the crash was your fault, even to be polite. Fault is decided later by the insurers using Ontario's Fault Determination Rules, not by what anyone says at the roadside.

If your car cannot be driven

If your car is not safe to drive, call your insurer's roadside help line or a tow company you choose. If a tow truck shows up on its own, you are allowed to say no and call your own. Do not sign any form at the scene that you do not understand, and keep every receipt for towing and storage. Your insurer can tell you which costs your policy covers.

Report it to your insurer promptly

Call your insurer as soon as you can, and aim to report the crash within seven days. Report it even if the damage looks small. A small dent can hide a bigger repair bill, and a late report can slow a claim down. Tell them plainly what happened, share the photos and the other driver's details, and keep a note of who you spoke to and when. If another driver was at fault, your own insurer usually handles your claim under Direct Compensation, the part of your policy that pays to fix your car and then sorts out the money with the other insurer.

What if the other driver has no insurance or leaves?

If the other driver has no insurance, or drives away before you can get their details, report it to police and to your insurer right away. The uninsured automobile coverage on your own policy may help with the damage. Give your insurer everything you have, even a partial plate number, and let them advise you on which part of your policy applies.

A crash is stressful, and the paperwork after it does not help. The steps above are the whole job: stay safe, report it when the law requires it, gather the details, and call your insurer promptly.
