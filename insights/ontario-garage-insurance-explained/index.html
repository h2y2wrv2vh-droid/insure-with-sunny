Run a Garage in Ontario? The Insurance You Actually Need

If you run a garage, your insurance is not one policy. It is a small set of coverages that work together, and each one covers a different kind of bad day.

Your shop holds other people's cars, expensive tools and customers walking through it, so one bad day can hit all three at once. Here is the set in plain language, and what to check before your next renewal.

Garage liability

Start with garage liability. If a customer slips in your waiting area, or your work damages someone else's property, garage liability typically helps with the cost of the injury or damage claim. It is the garage version of the liability coverage every business carries, shaped for a shop where vehicles come and go all day.

Garagekeepers: the cover that surprises most owners

Then comes the coverage that surprises most garage owners: garagekeepers. The moment a customer hands you their keys, their car is in your care, custody and control, and a standard liability policy typically does not cover property you are looking after for someone else. That gap is what garagekeepers fills. If a customer's car is damaged, stolen or burned while it is with you, on your lot or out on a test drive, garagekeepers typically answers. There are two forms of it, so ask which one you carry. One pays when the shop is legally responsible. The other pays regardless of fault, which matters when nobody can prove whose fault it was.

The OAP 4 form

In Ontario, these two coverages usually sit together in one standard policy form, called the OAP 4, the Ontario Garage Automobile Policy. It exists because a garage faces risks a normal business policy was never designed for: customer cars in your care, test drives and dealer plates. The form is standard across insurers, but limits and endorsements vary.

Your property, equipment and vehicles

Next, think about the things you own. Your building, lifts, tools and parts inventory are worth real money, and a fire or a break-in can take them all at once. Property and equipment cover typically helps repair or replace them. Ask about pollution cover too: oil, coolant and solvents can spill, and cleanup is typically a separate cover. Your own vehicles need attention too. A shop truck, tow truck or courtesy car needs commercial auto coverage, because a personal auto policy typically does not cover a vehicle used for business. That includes test drives: if a technician takes a customer's car out to check a repair, that drive needs to be covered. Service plates need the same attention. A service plate lets the shop legally drive a vehicle that is not registered to it, for example during a road test, so ask that the policy lists the plates you hold.

If the shop has to close

Then think about what happens if the shop has to close. A fire or a major incident can shut your bays for weeks, and the rent and wages keep coming while the income stops. Business interruption cover typically helps with some of the income you lose while you cannot work. One more thing sits outside the garage policy: injuries to your own staff. In Ontario, that goes through WSIB, the workplace safety system, not your garage insurance.

Three questions before renewal

Before your policy renews, ask three questions. First, has your shop changed since last year? More bays, EV work, more staff or more cars stored overnight all change what you need. Second, are your limits still enough for the cars you hold now? A policy written for ten cars a day may not fit a shop that holds thirty. Third, does your policy reflect how you actually work, including test drives, overnight storage and loaner cars? If any answer is no, that is the time for a second look, not after a claim.
