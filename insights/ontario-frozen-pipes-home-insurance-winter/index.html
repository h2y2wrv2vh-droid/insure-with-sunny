Frozen Pipes: How to Protect Your Ontario Home Before the Cold Hits

When water in a pipe freezes, it expands and can split the pipe. Here is how to keep that from happening to your Ontario home, and what your policy usually covers if it does.

When the temperature drops, water in your pipes can freeze, expand, and split the pipe. You might not notice anything until it thaws, and then water runs through ceilings, down walls, and into floors. A burst pipe can turn into a big repair bill and weeks of disruption. The good news is that most frozen pipes are preventable, and the fixes are simple.

Keep the heat on

The most common reason pipes freeze is a home that got too cold. Never turn the heat off completely in winter, even if you are away for a week. Keep it at a steady, modest temperature so the pipes stay above freezing. The extra heating cost is small next to the cost of a burst pipe and the water damage that follows.

Insulate the spots that freeze first

Pipes in attics, crawl spaces, garages, and inside exterior walls freeze first, because those are the coldest parts of the house. Foam pipe insulation is inexpensive and takes an afternoon to put on. Pay special attention to plumbing on outside walls, like a kitchen sink, which is a classic freeze point.

Shut off the water outside

Before the first hard freeze, disconnect garden hoses, shut off outdoor taps from inside the house, and drain the lines. A hose left connected can trap water in the pipe, and when that water freezes it can burst the line inside your wall, where you will not notice it until spring.

Know where your main shutoff is

If a pipe does burst, every minute of running water adds damage. Everyone in your household should know where the main water shutoff valve is and how to turn it. If you do not know where yours is, go find it today. It takes two minutes, and it is one of the most useful two minutes in homeownership.

Going away? Arrange check-ins

If you travel during heating season, ask a neighbour, friend, or family member to stop in regularly. A furnace can fail on day two of a two-week trip, and an unchecked home can freeze. Many home policies also have conditions about how often someone must check on your home while you are away in winter, so confirm what your policy asks for before you leave.

Open cabinet doors in a cold snap

During very cold weather, open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls. That lets warm room air reach the pipes, right where freezing is most likely. It costs nothing and takes seconds.

What does home insurance cover?

Here is the plain version. If a pipe bursts suddenly, that is typically a covered claim under a standard home policy, including the water damage it causes. But damage from a slow leak that went on for weeks, or from pipes that froze because the heat was off or the home was left unchecked, is often not covered. Insurers expect reasonable upkeep, so prevention protects your coverage as well as your home.

A quick checklist before winter sets in

The list is short, and an afternoon is enough to work through it.

  • Disconnect hoses, shut off outdoor taps, and drain the lines
  • Insulate exposed pipes in attics, crawl spaces, and garages
  • Find your main shutoff and show everyone in the house
  • Arrange check-ins if you travel during heating season
  • Keep the heat on
