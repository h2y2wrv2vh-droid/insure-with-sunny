Hosting Thanksgiving Dinner? What Your Home Policy Covers If a Guest Gets Hurt

If you are hosting Thanksgiving this year, here is the part of your home insurance worth understanding before the doorbell rings: your personal liability coverage. Most homeowners and tenants carry it without thinking much about it, yet a house full of guests is exactly the situation it exists for.

What Personal Liability Coverage Actually Does

Personal liability is the section of your home or tenant policy that responds when you are legally responsible for injury to someone else or damage to someone else's property. If a guest is hurt at your home and you are found liable, this coverage typically pays for their medical costs, your legal defence, and any settlement or judgment, up to your policy limit.

A few things people get wrong about it. First, it is not only for dramatic accidents. The most common liability claims are ordinary: a guest slips on a wet front step or trips over an uneven walkway stone. Second, it usually applies away from home too, not just on your property. Third, the limit matters more than most people realize, which we will come back to.

The Hosting Scenarios Worth Thinking About

Thanksgiving packs a lot of liability exposure into one evening: extra people, often older relatives and small children, moving through a home after dark while October rain or early frost makes steps slippery.

Slips and falls are the big one. As the host, you are expected to keep your property reasonably safe: clear walkways, a secure handrail, working outdoor lights, and a plan for wet boots by the door. If a guest falls on a hazard you knew about, or should have known about, and did nothing, that is the classic fact pattern behind a liability claim.

Then alcohol. Ontario courts have recognized that social hosts can face liability in some circumstances when a guest drives impaired after drinking at their home. The law is narrower than many assume and every case turns on its facts, so decide the ride situation before the wine opens: keys in a bowl, spare beds made up, rides arranged.

Food safety rounds out the list. Liability coverage typically responds to claims of negligence, so proper thawing, cooking temperatures, and not leaving food out for hours is both good hosting and good risk management.

Check Your Limit Before the Big Day

Here is the part most people skip: the dollar limit. Many Ontario home policies start with a $1 million personal liability limit as the standard base, which sounds like plenty until you add up medical care, lost income, and legal fees in a serious injury claim.

Raising your liability limit is usually one of the least expensive policy changes you can make. Moving from $1 million to $2 million typically costs very little for the protection it adds, and an umbrella policy stacks another layer on top. Changes must be in place before an incident, so call your broker this week.

Also confirm who is covered. The liability section typically covers you, your spouse, and resident relatives, and tenant policies carry liability too, so renters hosting dinner are part of this conversation. Hosting at a rented hall instead of your home is a different situation worth a separate question to your broker.

A Host's Prevention Checklist

Insurance responds after something happens. Prevention decides whether it happens at all.

Clear the approach. Sweep leaves and debris off walkways and steps, have salt or sand ready if frost is forecast, and fix the wobbly handrail now.

Light everything. Guests arrive after dark in October, so porch, pathway, and stairwell lights should all work.

Manage indoor hazards. Wet boots need a mat and tray, sliding rugs need grip pads, and small children warrant a scan at their eye level for sharp corners.

Plan the alcohol. Rides decided before dinner, not during dessert. Keep a taxi or rideshare number handy and make the spare room genuinely available.

Secure the pets. Even friendly dogs get overexcited with a house full of people. A separate room with water and a bed keeps everyone relaxed.

If Someone Does Get Hurt

Get them medical attention first. Document what happened while it is fresh, with photos of the area, and notify your broker or insurer promptly, since most policies require timely notice of potential claims. Avoid debating fault at the scene.
